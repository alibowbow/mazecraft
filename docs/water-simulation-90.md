# Water Simulation 90 — architecture and acceptance notes

This upgrade keeps MazeCraft's existing worker-owned, mass-conserving hydraulic
network as the authoritative solver and adds a bounded sub-grid GPU surface
simulation for the visual detail that a one-value-per-maze-cell state cannot
represent.

## Why the former result looked blocky

The hydraulic snapshot intentionally stores one depth and one velocity vector
per active maze cell. That is appropriate for fast, deterministic route-scale
flow, but it cannot by itself produce a continuous wetting front, transported
foam, breaking waves, or small ripples around corners. The previous history
pass also only accumulated and decayed foam in place, so foam looked attached
to cells rather than to moving water.

## Physics upgrade

The hydraulic edge solve now uses a pressure/gravity predictor followed by an
implicit linear-plus-quadratic drag solve. The quadratic equation is solved in
closed form, so drag cannot overshoot through zero and reverse an edge merely
because a time step or resistance is large.

Two wetting/drying stabilizers are applied per portal:

- a depth-dependent resistance multiplier for thin films;
- a local velocity limit based on `Fr * sqrt(g h)`, bounded by the existing
  absolute velocity ceiling.

The control-volume update, source and outlet boundaries, outflow limiter,
fixed 1/120 s worker step, and mass diagnostics remain authoritative and
unchanged in purpose.

## GPU sub-grid surface

High quality mode promotes the existing ping-pong history target into a
four-channel state field:

| Channel | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| R | semi-Lagrangian, flow-advected foam |
| G | signed local surface displacement |
| B | signed vertical wave velocity |
| A | turbulence and breaking-wave energy |

The history resolution is chosen once per maze. It uses up to 12 texels per
maze cell while keeping the longest side at or below 512 texels. The visual
solve advances at fixed 1/60 s substeps, catches up by at most 0.25 s, and uses
no new dependency or external asset.

The surface shader reconstructs the low-resolution hydraulic field manually
with four nearest-filtered taps. Each neighboring tap is accepted only when the
static topology atlas contains a connected portal route from the fragment's
owning cell. This smooths open passages without blending depth or velocity
through a closed wall.

The history pass samples the owning hydraulic cell without cross-cell bilinear
interpolation. The same topology texture is then injected into the history
shader for every renderer, so foam and wave transport can cross a cell edge
only when a real open portal exists and the wetness/velocity continuity checks
also pass. Closed-wall protection therefore does not depend on flow-direction
heuristics alone.

This design avoids requiring linear filtering for floating-point hydraulic
textures on constrained WebGL2 implementations. Half-float history targets are
used when color-buffer support is present; normalized 8-bit RGBA remains the
fallback.

## Surface shading

The water shader now combines:

- topology-aware smoothed depth and velocity sampling, eliminating cell
  plateaus while preserving closed walls;
- physically readable depth displacement rather than a nearly flat blue sheet;
- sub-grid height displacement and derivative normals;
- flow-aligned broad waves plus turbulence-coupled micro detail;
- Beer-Lambert-style spectral absorption and in-scattering, tuned so shallow
  water stays pale while deeper water moves toward blue-green;
- restrained Fresnel reflection, glitter, shallow caustic interference, and
  subsurface crest light;
- persistent foam generated by convergence, shear, hydraulic edges, impact,
  and wave energy, then advected with the velocity field.

The enhancement is installed as a narrowly scoped Three.js compile hook. It
only touches MazeCraft's water surface material and its high-resolution history
material, identified by their existing uniform signatures. A renderer-scoped
weak map pairs each history pass with the correct topology texture without
adding a global asset or changing the runtime API.

## Lifecycle and compatibility invariants

- Pause does not advance the worker, interpolation, GPU history, particles, or
  camera.
- Restart clears both ping-pong targets to the neutral signed state.
- Dispose frees both render targets, shader material, and full-screen geometry.
- Low quality mode retains the procedural fallback.
- No new package, API, network request, asset, or storage format is introduced.
- Existing maze editing, save/share/export, 2D solver, and standalone HTML paths
  are outside the change surface.

## Acceptance rubric

The result is considered ready for the 90-point target when browser visual QA
confirms all of the following:

1. No visible square cell plateaus in moving water at normal camera distance.
2. Foam travels downstream, gathers at compression/impact zones, and decays
   instead of blinking in place.
3. Closed walls show no visible cross-wall water or foam transfer.
4. Pools remain comparatively calm; fast corners and impacts carry more detail.
5. Depth reads as pale/transmissive when shallow and blue-green when deeper,
   without a full-screen cyan overlay.
6. Pause captures remain identical and restart starts from a neutral surface.
7. Auto quality stays responsive on the existing mobile threshold, and low
   quality remains functional.
8. Hydraulic mass error stays within the existing solver tolerance.

## Research basis

- Three.js `GPUComputationRenderer`: float RGBA ping-pong computation pattern
  used as the implementation reference for the history pass.
- Chentanez & Müller, *Real-Time Simulation of Large Bodies of Water with
  Small Scale Details*: advecting small-scale detail along a coarse velocity
  field.
- Kellomäki, *Real-Time Virtual Pipes Simulation and Modeling for Small-Scale
  Shallow Water*: virtual-pipe flow with stable viscosity treatment.
- NVIDIA research on colored transparency, refraction, and caustics: optical
  cues were adapted conservatively for a single-pass WebGL surface.

The solver remains a reduced-order game simulation, not a CFD validation tool.
The architecture deliberately spends precision where the player can see it
while preserving deterministic, bounded browser performance.
